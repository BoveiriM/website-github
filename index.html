About

A systems perspective.

I am a PhD researcher at Delft University of Technology, working at the intersection of optimization, control theory and reinforcement learning. Based in the Delft Center for Systems and Control and affiliated with the Delft AI Energy Lab, I study how algorithms can learn from limited data while maintaining reliable behavior in uncertain dynamical systems.

My research connects mathematical analysis with algorithm design and numerical evaluation. In reinforcement learning, I investigate variance reduction and sample complexity, asking how to obtain accurate value estimates with fewer observations. In adaptive control, I study how the geometry of an estimation method influences convergence and transient performance. This includes mirror-descent updates that exploit structured uncertainty in high-dimensional models, normalized parameter estimation laws, and the use of statistical prior information.

My earlier work examines nonlinear output-feedback control and adaptive compensation for actuator failures through Riemannian and contraction metrics. Across these projects, the recurring questions are how to represent uncertainty, how to use available structure, and how to establish explicit performance guarantees. The publications below connect each research direction to its assumptions, methods and results.

I am interested in opportunities that connect this research to optimization, control engineering and AI/ML, including applications in semiconductor technology. I bring a research perspective centered on precise problem formulation, careful analysis and reproducible numerical experiments. I welcome conversations about research collaborations and roles involving learning algorithms, dynamical systems and decisions under uncertainty.

Selected projects

Control & estimation

Adaptive control at scale

Problem
Large parameter matrices can make transient control performance depend strongly on ambient dimension.
Method
Mirror-descent adaptive laws use parameter geometry, including sparsity and low rank, with a non-Euclidean Polyak-type step size.
Result
The paper establishes asymptotic state convergence and regret bounds with at most logarithmic dimension dependence under its assumptions.

Reinforcement learning

Learning with fewer samples

Problem
Noisy updates can increase the number of observations needed to estimate an optimal Q-function.
Method
Cascade Q-learning combines a cascade variance-reduction scheme with direct variance reduction in the synchronous generative-model setting.
Result
The analysis establishes minimax-optimal sample complexity and instance optimality for policy evaluation, supported by numerical experiments.
Read the research ↗

Control & estimation

Faster adaptive dynamics

Problem
Slow state decay can limit the transient performance of an adaptive system.
Method
Normalized parameter estimation laws strengthen integrability guarantees, with momentum-based higher-order extensions.
Result
The method applies to CLF-based certainty-equivalence controllers under the paper’s assumptions and is evaluated in numerical studies.

Control & estimation

Putting prior knowledge to work

Problem
Controllers for populations of similar systems can have useful prior information about their unknown parameters.
Method
A parameter estimation law favors high-probability regions of the parameter space.
Result
Numerical studies show improved average parameter estimates and transient behavior in the studied settings.
