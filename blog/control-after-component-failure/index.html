Fault-tolerant systems

Keeping a system on track after a fault

Thinking about actuator failures through the relationship between adaptation, redundancy and system performance.

When the system changes

An actuator failure changes how a control input affects a physical system. A controller designed around nominal behavior may therefore need to adapt when actuator effectiveness is reduced.

Compensation and continued operation

My research on adaptive actuator failure compensation studies how control can respond to these changes. In over-actuated systems, redundancy provides additional possibilities for maintaining the desired behavior.

Reliability requires context

Fault compensation connects control theory to questions of operational reliability. The assumptions on available measurements, redundancy and the type of failure matter. A mathematical method provides a foundation; applying it to real equipment also requires system-specific verification.
